Alternative Hypoglycemic Medication

Abstract: The research investigates the potential of Costus igneus, commonly known as the insulin plant,
as an alternative hypoglycemic medication. The study employs a developmental research design to
evaluate the acceptability and effectiveness of Costus igneus tea in managing hyperglycemia. The
methodology includes five phases: gathering of Costus igneus leaves, preparation of Costus igneus leaves,
packaging of dried Costus igneus leaves, production of Costus igneus tea, and sensory evaluation. To
assess the acceptability of Costus igneus tea as a convenient alternative for commercial hypoglycemic
medication, the researchers specifically conducted a 9-point Hedonic Rating Scale and Descriptive Rating
Test with fifteen (15) evaluators consisting of TLE teachers, science teachers, and parents. The findings
indicate that the visual presentation and sensory attributes of the tea significantly influence consumer
acceptability, suggesting that the appearance of the product plays a crucial role in its marketability. The
results demonstrate that Costus igneus tea is not only a viable alternative for individuals with
hyperglycemia but also possesses favorable sensory qualities that enhance its appeal. The study highlights
the importance of traditional herbal remedies in contemporary health practices and encourages further
exploration of Costus igneus as a natural hypoglycemic agent. Overall, this research contributes to the
growing body of evidence supporting the use of herbal teas in diabetes management, emphasizing the
need for more accessible and natural treatment options.

Keywords: Alternative medicine, Costus igneus, herbal tea, hypoglycemic medication, sensory evaluation

I. INTRODUCTION
Diabetes has continuously increased over time and has affected many people across the world. According to
the IDF Diabetes Atlas (2021), among adults aged 20 to 79 years of age, 10.5% have diabetes. Between the
years 2000 and 2019, there has been a 3% increase in age-specific diabetic-related deaths (World Health
Organization: Diabetes, 2023). Due to the cost of treatment, people end up not receiving the proper treatment for
their condition (Jimeno et al., 2021). Therefore, it is essential to determine a safe, efficient, and cost-effective
alternative to conventional medication.

In this context, Rahman et al. (2022) stated that plant-based medications are believed to be at the top of the
list as a preventive measure for diseases and their related complications. Plant-based medication, also known as
herbal medicine, is not a new treatment since, from generation to generation, the knowledge about herbal
medicine has been practiced worldwide. Over time, various herbal medicines have been used, developed, and
discovered despite their risks (Tolentino et al., 2019). As stated by Pang et al. (2019), herbal medicine is a good
complementary and alternative treatment for type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) because of its holistic quality that
is capable of treating T2DM in various ways such as through anti-inflammatory, anti-oxidation, blood lipid
regulation, and anti-glucose properties. Additionally, tea contains bioactive properties such as flavonoids and
alkaloids, which have effects that lower blood glucose levels.
According to Zhang et al. (2022), multiple studies have shown that herbal tea has effectively improved
glucose metabolism disorders in individuals with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2 DM). One traditional plant that
has recently garnered global acceptance is the insulin plant. The herbaceous plant named Costus igneus (insulin
plant), belonging to the family Costaceae and native to Central and South America, is commonly referred to as
the "insulin plant" because of its properties that aid in building up the insulin in the human body by
strengthening β-cells of the pancreas in the body (Godi, 2019 and Vijay et al., 2023). Diabetic people have
consumed the leaves of this plant because it is believed to lower blood glucose levels, which has been confirmed
by diabetic people in the past to truly decrease an individual's blood glucose levels (Vijay et al., 2023).
Thus, in this study, the researchers will create a Costus igneus tea and test its overall characteristics:
appearance, aroma, taste, and texture, if they are recommendable as a convenient way of consumption.
Moreover, it is recommended as an alternative hypoglycemic medication. However, it is merely intended to be
an alternative form of ready-made hypoglycemic medication and not a replacement for it. The results of this
study not only focus on those individuals who suffer from hyperglycemia but also on everyone, especially those
who wish to avoid this condition or any other complications caused by hyperglycemia.

II. METHODOLOGY
The study used a development research design through a quantitative approach. The developmental research
design facilitated the systematic creation and refinement of the herbal tea. The quantitative research allowed for
the objective measurement and statistical analysis of the tea’s characteristics using numerical data. The study
was conducted in four (4) phases: (1) Preparation of Costus igneus (insulin plant) leaves, (2) Costus igneus tea
production, (3) Basic Nutritional Facts Analysis, and (4) Sensory evaluation. The study aimed to provide the
characteristics of the resulting product in terms of appearance, aroma, taste, and texture of Costus igneus tea as
an alternative hypoglycemic medication.
In this study, the researchers used an adapted questionnaire from Romano et al. (2024) divided into two (2)
parts. Part I was the Hedonic Rating Scale, in which a numerical rating from 1 to 9 will determine the rate of
acceptability of the product, in which 1 indicates “Dislike Extremely” and 9 indicates “Like Extremely”.
Additionally, the researchers also used the Descriptive Rating Test (Part II) with a 4-point scale with 1
indicating “Highly Not Recommendable” and 4 as “Highly Recommendable”; this was used to provide a detailed
description of the product’s sensory evaluation.
This study used quota and purposive sampling to select evaluators, including food experts, TLE teachers, and
science teachers of St. Paul University Surigao. This sampling method ensured an accurate group of evaluators
was selected to participate in this study in order to gather relevant responses about the product. Before
participating, they were provided with relevant information about the evaluation process and gave their consent
to assess the product.

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION
This chapter presents the results and discusses the data obtained from the responses of fifteen (15)
respondents. The data were carefully analyzed, presented, discussed, and interpreted according to the perimeter
of this research to answer the problem sought for this study.

Table 1. Basic Nutritional Facts Analysis
The nutritional facts analysis of Costus igneus (insulin plant) tea are listed in Table 1. The parameters are
described from largest to smallest, along with certain essential information about the product's nutritional value
and how it will affect the health of the consumer and market demands. Based on the results, it can be observed
that Costus igneus tea has a high carbohydrate content of 70.4%, making it a potential energy source for those
relying on carbohydrates for daily fuel. Despite this, it contains bioactive compounds like corosolic acid and
flavonoids that enhance insulin sensitivity, implying hypoglycemic benefits. This suggests that the tea could
support energy needs while aiding in blood sugar regulation, which is particularly relevant in the Philippines,
where carbohydrate-rich diets are standard. Clemente-Suárez et al. (2022) state that foods high in carbohydrates
are an important part of a healthy diet since they provide the body with glucose to support bodily functions and
physical activity. This finding was also supported by Mohanta et al. (2022), who stated that herbal materials
offer a rich source of structurally diverse and bioactive polysaccharides.
The protein content of Costus igneus (insulin plant) tea is considerably high at 12.0%, determined using the
Kjeldahl method. This suggests that the tea is a good plant-based protein source, supporting muscle maintenance
and overall health. Its high protein value implies potential benefits for individuals shifting to a plant-based diet,
offering an alternative to animal protein sources. In their article, Ma et al. (2022) stated that consumers are
moving towards a more plant-based diet. However, some consumers avoid common plant proteins such as soy
and gluten due to their potential allergenicity. Therefore, alternative protein sources are being explored as
functional ingredients in foods. This implies that this product could be a novel potential source of protein. The
fiber content with a value of 24.5% was determined by an Ankom method. This presents the dietary benefits
accruable to the formulation. As fiber is essential in the human gut, this product impacts the body's glucose level,
improves gut health, and prevents chronic disease. In line with these findings, Xiet et al. (2020) explained that
soluble dietary fiber is prompted as an important part of reducing blood glucose, ameliorating insulin resistance
and controlling body weight.
Additionally, the ash content of Costus igneus (insulin plant) tea is 10.2%, indicating a rich mineral profile.
This reflects the presence of essential minerals that contribute to overall health and well-being. Its high mineral
content implies potential benefits for maintaining electrolyte balance and supporting various bodily functions.
As noted in the findings of Mayer et al. (202), micronutrient malnutrition is widespread and is linked with diets
low in fruit and vegetables. This signifies that a need for a convenient and healthy drinkable product that can be
available everywhere may aid this common problem. In this context, Ceccanti et al. (2021) added that minerals
in the human diet are essential for metabolism and human health.
The moisture content of Costus igneus tea is 4.70%, determined using the vacuum oven method. This method
involves applying high temperatures under reduced pressure to remove moisture effectively. The low moisture
content implies better shelf stability and reduced risk of microbial growth, enhancing the product's storage life.
Considering this, Zhang et al. (2022) justified that heat treatment is one of the most important methods to
improve foodstuffs' physicochemical properties and nutritional profile, resulting from changes in protein
structure and protein denaturation. Furthermore, the total sugar content of Costus igneus (insulin plant) tea is the
lowest among its components at 0.0848%, measured using the Munson-Walker Method. This low sugar level
suggests that the tea is suitable for individuals monitoring their sugar intake, such as those with diabetes. It also
implies that the tea can provide energy without significantly impacting blood glucose levels.
Low sugar is desirable because a high intake of sugars is associated with numerous diseases (WHO, 2022);
the organization also reported that excessive sugar intake, particularly in the form of sugar-sweetened beverages
(SSBs), contributes to various health risks, including obesity, dental caries, and chronic diseases. This result
makes the product appealing to diabetic and calorie-conscious individuals pursuing low-sugar snacks.

Table 3. General Acceptability of Costus igneus Tea through Hedonic Rating Scale

Table 3 shows the general acceptability of insulin plant tea using a descriptive rating test. Appearance
received the highest rating with a mean score of 3.73 and a standard deviation of 0.46, classified as "Strongly
Acceptable" (SA). This suggests that the tea's visual appeal significantly contributed to its positive perception,
indicating that the product is visually well-received and likely recommendable based on appearance alone. Song
et al. (2021) noted that appearance and flavor are crucial indicators of the tea's quality. Hence, having an
appearance as the most favored among all variables indicates that the product has a good advantage if pursued to
be sold in the market. However, Li et al. (2022) suggested that the dark color of some green teas can be
improved through technological experiments in the future.
The next highest mean score was for aroma (3.53, SD = 0.52), also considered "Strongly Acceptable,"
indicating that the tea was favorably received in terms of odor, albeit with less visual impact than previous
results. Given the positive reception of its aroma, the product is recommended as it appeals to consumers'
sensory preferences. The findings are consistent with Romano et al. (2024) that aroma, scoring 3.50, is
designated as Highly Recommended (HR) and contributes a mild yet pleasant scent, aligning with the subtle
nature of herbs. As Song et al. (2024) conducted, different scented teas provide various choices for consumers in
appearance, aroma, flavor, and others.
The mean for taste was 3.20 with a standard deviation of 0.68, rated as "Acceptable" (A), indicating that the
flavor of the tea met basic expectations but could be improved to enhance its appeal. Although there is room for
enhancement, the product is still recommendable based on its overall acceptability. Based on the study
conducted by Wang, Yue, & Tong (2021) showed that 22 chemical components had an important contribution
to the taste characteristics of yellow tea, and the chemical components that had an important influence on each
taste component were obtained.
Texture received a mean score of 3.27 with a standard deviation of 0.80, indicating that the tea's mouthfeel
was generally well-received and classified as "Strongly Acceptable." The relatively high acceptance suggests
that the texture contributed positively to the overall sensory experience. Based on this result, the product is
recommendable in terms of texture. The findings agree with that of Ambid et al. (2024) that texture is the least
favored characteristic of the developed product. However, it still falls within the recommended category. The
global average acceptability was 3.43 (SD = 0.47), classifying the tea as "Strongly Acceptable," revealing the
characteristics of tea in terms of smell and taste consumed, but with room for improvement of the sensory
profile of the product in order to enhance consumer acceptance.

IV. CONCLUSIONS
In conclusion, Costus igneus (insulin plant) is accepted and recommended as an alternative tea for
hypoglycaemic medication. Among all attributes, appearance was the most accepted and recommended, texture
was the least accepted, and taste was the least recommended.
