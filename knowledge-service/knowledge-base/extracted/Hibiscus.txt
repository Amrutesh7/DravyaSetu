ABSTRACT 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis L., commonly known as China rose, is a 
medicinal plant with a rich history of traditional use. This 
comprehensive review explores the diverse pharmacological properties 
of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis, including its antibacterial, antifungal, 
antidiabetic, and anticancer activities. Additionally, it exhibits 
antioxidant, anti-inflammatory, and wound healing effects, making it a 
valuable botanical resource for various healthcare needs. From its 
historical significance in ancient texts to its integration into 
contemporary Ayurvedic databases, Hibiscus rosa-sinensis holds 
promising potential for modern medicine and natural remedies. 
 
KEYWORDS: Hibiscus rosa-sinensis, China rose, medicinal plant,  
pharmacological properties, modern medicine, natural remedies. 
 
INTRODUCTION 
Throughout history, medicinal plants have been extensively utilized for various purposes due 
to their remarkable ability to synthesize chemical compounds that play a vital role in 
preventing numerous diseases, including cancer and diabetes. These plants boast a diverse 
array of chemical compounds, each serving essential biological functions and acting as a 
defense mechanism against predators such as fungi, insects, and herbivorous mammals. To 
date, scientists have isolated approximately 12,000 of these compounds, although this figure 
is believed to represent less than 10% of the total number of compounds present.[1,2] The 
potential of medicinal plants and their chemical constituents continues to be a subject of great 
interest and exploration in the field of medicine and natural remedies.

Medicinal plants can be classified into three significant sectors based on their usage. 
(a) Modern medicines rely on approximately 30 to 35 medicinal plant species. 
(b) Organized and codified traditional medicines, such as Ayurveda, Siddha, Unani, Amchi, 
and Tibetan systems, draw from a broader range of about 1,200 to 2,000 medicinal plant 
species. 
(c) Local health traditions encompass practices followed by villagers, folk healers, vaidyas, 
and tribal communities, utilizing an extensive array of over 8,000 species of medicinal plants 
primarily for essential healthcare needs.[3] 
 
Each of these sectors represents a unique approach to harnessing the therapeutic potential of 
medicinal plants, contributing to a diverse and rich landscape of traditional and modern 
healthcare systems. 
 
The Hibiscus rosa sinensis L. (Malvaceae) herb is originally native to China and is well-
known for its stunning, showy flowers. This shrub is widely cultivated in tropical regions as 
an ornamental plant, presenting various forms with flowers of different colors. Beyond its 
aesthetic appeal, Hibiscus also possesses medicinal properties, making it a key ingredient in 
numerous herbal teas. 
 
Among the different varieties, the red-flowered variety is particularly favored for its 
medicinal applications. Numerous studies have reported that different varieties of Hibiscus 
plants exhibit varying medicinal properties. However, this review primarily centers on 
exploring the therapeutic potential of the Hibiscus rosa sinensis plant and its wide-ranging 
applications in traditional and modern medicine. 
 
MATERIALS AND METHODS 
This paper includes an evidence-based overview of the pharmacological properties and

from different classical texts, spanning various historical periods. The present article provide

Etymology Of The Word[4] 
The word "Hibiscus" has interesting origins: Dioscorides associated it with the Marshmallow 
plant. "Hibiscus" refers to a plant belonging to the mallow family, thriving in warm climate 
tes, known for its striking, large, and brightly colored flowers. It also has applications in 
producing fibers or timber. 
 
The term "rosa-sinensis" was attributed to the deep-red flowered variety, believed to have 
originated in Asia, hence the name. 
 
Chronological Observations 
Vedic Period[5] 
The Hibiscus flowers find special use in Hindu deity worship, particularly in the reverence of 
Goddess Durga/Kali. The glowing complexion of the Devi is often compared to the beauty of 
the Hibiscus flower in the Lalitopaakhyaana. The Sun God is also associated with the 
Hibiscus flower, as seen in a well-known shloka that begins with 'Japa kusumasankarsham, 
kashyapeyammaha-dyuthim'. 
 
Samhita Period 
No references to Japa are found in texts from this period. 
 
Nighantu Kala[6-8] 
Several Nighantus, including Amarakosha, Paryayaratnamala, and Raja Nighantu, mention 
Japa. 
Raja Nighantu classifies it under karavira. Bhavaprakash Nighantu includes Japa in the 
Pushpavarga. Amarakosha refers to it as java in the Vanaushadhivarga. 
 
Adhunika Kala 
Japa is identified as Hibiscus rosa-sinensis Linn. in contemporary Ayurvedic databases, such 
as the Ayurvedic Pharmacopoeia of India. 
 
Detailed information on its Morphological features, Vernacular names, Microscopic features, 
Phytochemicals, and Therapeutic uses is available. Japa is listed in the karpasadi varga of 
Nighantu Adarsha. Pushpa Ayurveda explains its therapeutic uses. Various regional books,

Economic And Botanical Significance of Hibiscus 
Shoe Flower: The dried powder of Hibiscus is utilized as shoe shine or polish, hence earning 
it the name "shoe flower." Moreover, its dry flower powder is a common component in 
various herbal tea blends. 
 
Economic Importance: The mallow family holds considerable economic significance due to 
the abundance of fiber-yielding plants. Nearly all genera within this family possess some 
form of fiber-producing capability. 
 
Dye Production: Petals of Hibiscus are used to create dyes, adding to their practical 
applications. 
 
Landscaping: Hibiscus plants are often employed for hedges and screens, enhancing their 
role in landscaping. 
 
Cosmetics: The juice extracted from Hibiscus petals is utilized in China for shoe-blacking 
and mascaras, showcasing its diverse applications. 
 
Substitutes And Adulterants 
Adulteration: The drug powder of Hibiscus may sometimes be adulterated with 
H.schizopetalus Hook.f., H. mutabilis Linn., and Malvaviscusmollis DC. To distinguish the 
genuine drug, one can observe the presence of various types of trichomes. 
 
Culinary Use: Young leaves of Hibiscus occasionally serve as a substitute for spinach in 
culinary preparations. 
 
Parts Used And Dosage 
Parts Used 
: Flowers, leaves, and roots. 
Dosage 
: Typically, the powder is administered in quantities ranging from 5 to 10 
grams.

Geographical distribution 
The origin and historical distribution of the red Hibiscus remain a mystery. The genus 
Hibiscus comprises around 200 species, which are naturally found across tropical and 
subtropical regions. As a popular choice, Hibiscus is often cultivated as a garden ornamental, 
thriving in elevations from sea level up to 500 meters. However, it is sensitive to frost and 
may experience freezing in mild winters. Nonetheless, it exhibits resilience by regenerating 
from the base during spring. To ensure optimal growth, it flourishes in well-drained, fertile 
soil with abundant organic matter, basking in full sunlight. 
 
Pharmacological/ Biological Activities 
1. Antibacterial Activity 
Methanol extracts from H. rosa-sinensis leaves exhibited antibacterial activity against 
Pseudomonas aeruginosa, Escherichia coli, Enterobacter aerogenes, and Streptococcus 
pyogenes. The main compounds responsible for this activity were flavonoids, tannins, 
terpenoids, saponins, and alkaloids. Both aqueous and methanol extracts showed inhibition 
zones against various bacteria, with different magnitudes of inhibition. The extracts from the 
flowers and leaves were effective against Bacillus subtilis, E. coli, and S. aureus. The 
antibacterial activity increased with higher extract concentrations. The identified compounds 
in the extracts included cardiac glycosides, anthraquinones, phlobatanins, carbohydrates, 
phytosterols, proteins, glycosides, tannins, phenols, and flavonoids. Alkaloids and saponins 
were found in both extracts.[11-15] 
 
2. Antifungal Activity 
Previous studies have confirmed the noteworthy antifungal activity of Hibiscus rosa sinensis. 
Its leaves, roots, and flowers yield extracts that exhibit inhibitory effects against several 
fungi, including Candida albicans, Aspergillus niger, and Candida parapsilosis. The 
antifungal properties are attributed to the presence of bioactive compounds like flavonoids, 
tannins, terpenoids, saponins, and alkaloids.[16] 
 
Methanol leaf extracts of H. rosa sinensis exhibited notable inhibition against Aspergillus 
niger and Candida albicans. Ethyl alcohol extracts from the plant's flowers also displayed 
high zones of inhibition against Candida parapsilosis and Aspergillus niger.[17] Additionally, 
the plant's methanol extracts inhibited the growth of other fungi, including C. glabreta, A. 
flavus, and C. albicans, while the flower extract showed activity against various fungi except 
Aspergillus flavus.[18,19]

3. Reproductive Effects 
(a) Male Reproductive Effects 
Benzene chloroform and alcoholic extracts decreased spermatogenic elements and sperm 
count in mice.[20] Ethanol, chloroform, and ethyl acetate extracts caused a decrease in testis 
weight and sperm parameters in mice.[21] Oral administration of aqueous and alcoholic 
extracts did not significantly affect the reproductive organs of male rats.[22] 
 
(b). Female Reproductive Effects 
Benzene extract disrupted the estrous cycle and reduced the weight of ovaries, uterus, and 
pituitary gl in rats.[23] Benzene extract caused an irregular estrous cycle, atretic follicles, and 
absence of corpora lutea in mice.[24] Ethanolic and benzene extracts reduced glycogen content 
in the uterus of rats.[25] 
 
(c) Anti-Fertility Effects 
Benzene hot extracts from flowers, leaves, and stem barks of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis 
prevented pregnancy in female rats.[26] Benzene extract administered during early pregnancy 
caused anti-implantation effects and inhibited endometrial capillaries in mice.[27] Ethanolic 
extract of the roots showed anti-implantation and uterotropic activity in rats.[28] Benzene 
extract administered during early pregnancy led to a failure of implantation and affected 
uterine composition in mice.[29] 
 
(d) Effects on Diabetic Pregnancy 
Aqueous extract of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flowers had both deleterious and beneficial effects 
on maternal-fetal outcomes in diabetic rats.[30] Treatment with the extract improved maternal 
and fetal weights and reduced certain biochemical parameters in diabetic pregnant rats.[30] 
 
4. Antioxidant Effects 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flower extracts exhibit strong antioxidant activity. The ethanolic 
extract scavenged hydrogen peroxide effectively and contained alkaloids, tannins, steroids, 
glycosides, and flavonoids. Methanolic and ethanolic extracts showed DPPH radical 
scavenging activity, with the methanolic extract having higher phenolic and flavonoid 
contents. Aqueous stem extracts had higher DPPH scavenging activity. Water extracts of 
petals demonstrated significant DPPH scavenging activity due to their iron content.[31,32,33] 
Aqueous leaf extracts had slightly higher antioxidant activity than ethanol extracts. Different 
flower colors and leaf types had varying antioxidant activities. Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extract

5. Antidiabetic Activity 
Hibiscus rosa sinensis extracts have been found to possess significant anti-diabetic properties 
in various animal models and clinical trials. The alcoholic leaf extracts exhibited oral 
hypoglycemic effects in non-obese type I diabetic mice, leading to a significant reduction in 
blood glucose levels.[38] Additionally, root extracts demonstrated anti-diabetic effects in 
alloxan-induced type II diabetic rats.[39] Furthermore, ethanolic leaf extracts showed 
promising results in reducing blood glucose levels and providing renal and hepatic protection 
in streptozotocin-induced diabetic rats.[40,41] Even the ethanolic flower extract displayed anti-
hyperglycemic activity in alloxan-induced hyperlipidemic rats.[42,43,44] 
 
In clinical trials involving type II diabetic patients, H. rosa sinensis flower powder proved 
beneficial by reducing fasting and postprandial blood glucose levels, as well as lowering total 
cholesterol and triglyceride levels.[45] These combined findings highlight the potential of 
Hibiscus rosa sinensis extracts as effective and multi-faceted anti-diabetic agents. 
 
6. Antihyperlipidemic Activity 
The oral administration of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis Linn flower ethanolic extract at a dosage of 
500 mg/kg b.w was evaluated in hyperlipidemic rats induced by triton and atherogenic diet. 
In the triton model, the extract significantly lowered serum lipid levels, including total 
cholesterol, triglycerides, phospholipids, LDL, and VLDL, while increasing HDL levels. 
Similarly, in the atherogenic diet model, the extract significantly reduced serum lipid levels 
and increased HDL levels compared to control groups. These findings suggest the potential of 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extract in managing hyperlipidemia.[46] 
 
7. Anticancer Activity 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extract has been found to exhibit protective effects against tumor 
promotion in cancer development. In a study using mouse skin, the extract mitigated 
hyperproliferation and oxidative damage induced by benzoyl peroxide and ultraviolet 
radiation. Pretreatment with the extract restored levels of cellular protective enzymes, 
reduced malondialdehyde formation and hydrogen peroxide content, and decreased ornithine 
decarboxylase activity and thymidine incorporation in DNA.[47]

Furthermore, the chemo-preventive activity of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extract was investigated 
in mouse skin carcinogenesis induced by 7,12-dimethyl benz(a)anthracene (DMBA)/croton 
oil and 12-O-tetradecanoyl phorbol-13-acetate (TPA). Application of the extractbeforeo 
Crotonil resulted in a significant reduction in the number of tumors and the percentage of 
tumor-bearing mice. The extract also delayed the appearance of the first tumor. Additionally, 
pretreatment with the extract and the compound gentisic acid restored the levels of 
glutathione (GSH), antioxidant enzymes, and metabolizing enzymes, while reducing 
malondialdehyde formation and hydrogen peroxide content.[48] 
 
The cytotoxic activity of different extracts of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis was evaluated against 
cancer cell lines. Methanol extracts of the leaf showed the highest cytotoxic activity against 
leukaemic cancer cells, followed by petroleum ether and ethyl acetate extracts. Similarly, 
chloroform and aqueous extracts of the flower exhibited inhibition of cell viability against 
MCF-7 cell lines in a dose-dependent manner.[49] The aqueous extracts specifically inhibited 
melanoma cell growth without affecting non-transformed cells and contained low molecular 
weight growth inhibitory compounds.[50] 
 
8. Hair Growth Promoting Activity 
Hibiscus rosa sinensis extracts have shown hair growth-promoting activity in animal studies. 
The petroleum ether leaf extract increased hair length and density, while the hydrolcholic leaf 
extract improved hair length.[51,52] The extracts stimulated the anagen phase of hair follicles 
and showed potential comparable to minoxidil.[53,54] Additionally, the aqueous flower extract 
increased hair length in rats. Further research is needed to explore their mechanisms and 
potential applications for hair growth.[55] 
 
9. Antioxidant Effects 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extracts were evaluated for antioxidant potential. Methanol and 
ethanol extracts showed high phenolic and flavonoid contents, as well as significant DPPH 
scavenging and linoleic acid inhibition.[56] Flower extracts effectively scavenged hydrogen 
peroxide and superoxide radicals.[57] Leaves exhibited excellent antioxidant activity 
surpassing synthetic antioxidants.[58] Hibiscus rosa-sinensis displayed strong antioxidant and 
ferric-reducing abilities.[59] Petal extracts demonstrated effective antioxidant activity in 
various assays.[60]

10. Neuroprotective Activity 
The methanolic extract of H. rosa-sinensis roots exhibited analgesic, sedative, and anxiolytic 
effects in mice and rats. It contained phytochemicals like tannins, flavonoids, saponins, and 
glycosides.[61] Another study showed that the methanol root extract improved learning, 
memory deficits, and anxiety induced by cerebral ischemia in rats.[62] The petroleum ether 
flower extract enhanced spatial memories in rats with cognitive decline.[63] Additionally, the 
ethanolic extract of H. rosa-sinensis roots displayed significant antidepressant activity in mice 
and rats.[64] 
 
11. Immunomodulatory Activity 
Hydro-alcoholic extract of dried Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flowers showed immunological 
effects, while the extract of Euphorbia neriifolia had a stronger impact on immune 
components.[65] The aqueous extract of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis increased plaque-forming cells, 
antibody titer, and DTH response in mice while altering IL-1alpha and IL-2 levels.[66] 
 
12. Wound Healing Activity 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flower extract demonstrated wound healing activity, reducing 
epithelialization time and wound area.[67] The leaf extracts also showed significant tensile 
strength and improved histological features in wound healing.[68] In mice, the extract 
enhanced wound closure and strength. It was effective in promoting healing in both thermal 
and chemical burn wounds.[69] 
 
13. Gastro-protective Activity 
The aqueous-ethanolic extract of Hibiscus rosa sinensis aerial parts provided mucosal 
protection in the gastric tissues of rats. It reduced gastric secretion and acidity while 
increasing pH.[70] The extract showed a significant decrease in ulcer index and exhibited 
gastro-protective activity in various ulcer models. The presence of carbohydrates, sterols, 
flavonoids, glycosides, and tannins contributed to its effects.[71] 
 
14. Cardio-protective Activity 
The administration of H. rosa-sinensis leaf extracts lowered blood pressure in both 
hypertensive and non-hypertensive rats. The extract showed potential kidney function 
interference.[72] In vitro studies demonstrated cardio-protective effects, improving post-
ischemic recovery and reducing ventricular premature beats and tachycardia.[73]

15. Anticonvulsant Activity: 
The ethanolic extracts of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flowers showed anticonvulsant activity. 
Further fractionation indicated that the acetone-soluble part of the ethanolic extract possessed 
this activity. The fraction protected animals from various types of convulsions induced by 
different substances, such as maximum electroshock, electrical kindling, pentylenetetrazole, 
lithium-pilocarpine, and electrical kindling. It also counteracted the effects of D-
amphetamine and enhanced pentobarbitone-induced sleep. Additionally, it increased the 
levels of gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) and serotonin in the brain.[77] 
 
16. Antidepressant Effects: 
The crude ethanolic extract of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flowers exhibited an antidepressant 
effect. The study used forced-induced swimming test (FST), tail suspension test (TST), and 
open field test (OFT) as parameters. The extract showed a significant dose-dependent 
decrease in immobility time in FST and TST. However, it did not produce significant effects 
on OFT. The extract also affected the levels of monoamine oxidase A (MAOA) and 
monoamine oxidase B (MAOB), with the 250mg/kg dose significantly influencing 
MAOB.[78] 
 
17. Anti-inflammatory Activity 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extracts have demonstrated anti-inflammatory activity in different 
models. 
Rectal administration of H. rosa-sinensis hydroalcoholic leaf extracts reduced colon ulcer 
areas in rats with colitis.[79] Ethanol extracts of H. rosa-sinensis flowers and leaves reduced 
paw edema in rats induced by carrageenan.[80] Phytochemicals present in the extracts, such as 
steroids, polyphenols, alkaloids, and flavonoids, likely contribute to their anti-inflammatory 
effects.[79] 
 
18. Antipyretic Activity 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis extracts showed antipyretic activity: Aqueous root extract reduced 
rectal temperature in rats with yeast-induced pyrexia. The extract exhibited analgesic effects 
in the tail-flicking test.[81] Aqueous leaf extract lowered rectal temperature in mice.[82] These 
findings indicate the potential of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis as an antipyretic agent.

19. Antiparasitic Activity 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis leaf methanol extract exhibited significant antiparasitic effects against 
Hymenolepis diminuta. 
 
In vitro study: The extract paralyzed the worms within 3 hours and caused mortality within 
4 hours at a concentration of 40 mg/ml. 
 
In vivo study: The highest dose (800 mg/kg) of the extract reduced eggs per gram (EPG) 
count by 66.55% and worm count by 75.00% in infected rats.[83] 
 
20. Fibrinolytic Effects 
The aqueous extract of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis was investigated for its fibrinolytic effect, and 
the specific fraction responsible for this activity was identified. The results demonstrated that 
the extract exhibited potent fibrinolytic activity without causing haemolysis. Among the 
fractions tested, fraction 5 showed the highest and most significant fibrinolytic activity.[84] 
 
21. Anti-haemolytic Effects 
The anti-haemolytic activity of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis flowers was assessed in vitro. The 
flower extract demonstrated a significant reduction in hydrogen peroxide-induced haemolysis 
and lipid peroxidation, indicating its ability to protect erythrocytes from damage.[57] 
 
22. Urinary Effects 
The extract inhibits crystal aggregation and interferes with the early stages of stone 
formation, suggesting potential for treating urolithiasis.[85] Urinary volume and electrolyte 
extraction: In rats, the extract increases urinary volume and the excretion of sodium and 
chloride ions.[86] 
 
23. Hepato-protective Activity 
The ethanolic extract of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis leaves alleviated the liver from piroxicam-
induced toxicity in mice. The extract was administered orally at 30 mg/kg for 15 days. The 
liver enzymes (alanine transaminase, aspartate transaminase, and alkaline phosphatase) in the 
blood were significantly lower in the extract-treated group than in the piroxicam-treated 
group. The extract also enhanced the antioxidant status of the liver by reducing lipid 
peroxidation and increasing the activities of superoxide dismutase, catalase, and glutathione 
peroxidase, which showed its potential to prevent oxidative stress-mediated hepatic 
damage.[87]

24. Effects On Colitis 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis hydroalcoholic leaf extract showed ameliorative effects in acetic acid-
induced colitis in rats. It reduced oxidative stress, colonic inflammation markers (NO and 
TNF-α), and increased antioxidant levels (SOD and GSH) at doses of 100 and 200 mg/kg.[88] 
 
25. Antitussive Effects 
The methanolic extract of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis demonstrated antitussive activity in a cough 
model induced by citric acid. It significantly reduced the number of coughing episodes, 
similar to the effect of codeine.[89] 
 
26. Antiviral Activity 
The ethanol (80%) extract of freeze-dried plant showed equivocal antiviral activity against 
coxsackie B2, measles, and poliovirus I in cell culture at varying concentrations. However, it 
was inactive against adenovirus, herpes virus type I, and semlickiforest virus in terms of 
plaque inhibition.[90] 
 
DISCUSSION 
Hibiscus rosa-sinensis, also known as Japa, is a plant with ornamental value that has been 
grown in India since ancient times. It is widely distributed across the country and has various 
pharmacological activities. The plant has been described in many classical Ayurvedic texts, 
except the Samhitas. It can act as an antibacterial and antifungal agent, thanks to the 
flavonoids, tannins, terpenoids, saponins, and alkaloids in its extracts. These compounds 
make it a potential natural remedy for infections. Moreover, the plant can affect reproductive 
health, lower blood sugar and cholesterol levels, and inhibit cancer cells, suggesting a broad 
spectrum of therapeutic uses. 
 
The antioxidant and neuroprotective activities of Hibiscus rosa-sinensis suggest its potential 
in managing oxidative stress and supporting brain health. The plant's ability to promote 
wound healing, gastro-protection, and cardio-protection further adds to its medicinal value. 
However, caution is necessary, as high doses may lead to hepatotoxicity and nephrotoxicity, 
emphasizing the need for proper dosage regulation and safety considerations. 
 
CONCLUSION 
In conclusion, Hibiscus rosa-sinensis exhibits diverse pharmacological activities that hold 
promise for natural therapeutic applications. Its extracts demonstrate potent antibacterial and
