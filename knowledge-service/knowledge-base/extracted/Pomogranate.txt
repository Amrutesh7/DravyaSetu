1
Origin and Habitat
Pomegranate, often referred to as “Fruit of the heaven” is native to Iran and
north western Himalayas and has been grown throughout the Mediterranean Asia,
Africa and Europe from centuries. Pomegranate ﬁnds mention in various religious
manuscripts. Pomegranate tree is well suited for growth in temperate as well as
sub-tropical regions and requires cool winter and hot summer. The plant is drought
tolerant and prefers semi-arid climate for its successful cultivation. A persistent expo-
sure to sub-freezing temperature is unsuitable for propagation of pomegranate tree.
Moreover, the temperature of 12 °C or above is favourable for its fruitful thriving.
Pomegranate blossoms well at an altitude of 500–1850 m above sea level and requires
a continuous supply of 5.3 mm of water per day. Pomegranate prefers deep, loamy
soil with 5.5–7.2 pH and salt concentration of 2000–25000 ppm. However, trees can
tolerate sandy soils with moderate pH (Aulakh & Sur, 1999).
2
Morphological Characteristics
Pomegranate tree is a member of family “Punicaceae” which comprises only one
genus “Punica” and two species. The plant is a deciduous, evergreen attractive shrub
6–10 m in height and long lived. The tree bears thorny multiple branches. The leaves
of the plant are evergreen, 1–10 cm long and short stemmed are placed as a cluster
of 5–6 on branches. The ﬂowers, 3 cm wide, are characterized by red colour, pointed
sepals and numerous stamens. Two to seven ﬂowers are present at the periphery
of each branch. The hexagonal-shaped fruit is 6–12 cm wide and weighs around
200 g. The fruit is crowned by thick tubular calyx. The rind of the fruit is red, hard
and leathery. The edible fruit, sarcotesta, is a berry which comprises of 600 arils (a
casing for seeds) under a thick skin. The ripe fruit attains red colour. The arils are
placed in several bunches separated by membranous walls and white spongy tissue
(Ataseven Isık & Celik, 2006).
3

hectares is the largest producer of pomegranate (Kushwaha et al., 2013) while China
and Turkey with 0.3 million metric tonnes and 0.22 million metric tonnes, respec-
tively stands at second and third position in worldwide pomegranate production. Over
the years, pomegranate production in India has increased by 20–25% causing it to top
the list of pomegranates producing countries. Earlier the scenario was different, Iran
was the largest producer. Besides Iran, pomegranate grows in Turkey, Spain, China,
Egypt. In India, Maharashtra contributes maximum to total production with 71% of

Nutritional Value and Chemical Composition
Pomegranate is a nutrient dense fruit with low caloriﬁc value in comparison to other
fruits and is a source of all essential and easily digestible nutrients, which refreshes
physically or mentally tired people. It is a diet full of nourishment for adolescents,
ageing/elderly or sick persons and pregnant women. It has the power to cure hunger
and quench thirst (Kushwaha et al., 2013) (Table 1).
• Carbohydrate
Pomegranate is relatively a good source of carbohydrates. A 100 g of pomegranate
arils provide around 18.7 g of carbohydrates that too in the form of sugars.
Pomegranate is considered a high GI fruit. The total sugar content of Pomegranate
is 10 g/100 g of fruit weight with glucose being the predominant (5 mg/100 g)
followed by fructose. Sorbitol (4.9 mg/100 g) is also present in good amounts and is
responsible for laxative effect (Singh & Sethi, 2003).
• Fibre
Pomegranate is a medium source of ﬁbre with the average ﬁbre content of fruit
4 g/100 g ﬁbre of the fruit consists of non-digestible carbohydrates and lignans. The
major ﬁbre in fruit is pectin (1.4%). The soluable and insoluable dietary ﬁbre content
of pomegranate is around 0.64 and 0.18%, respectively (Melgarejo & Artes, 2000).

• Protein
Unlike most of the fruits, pomegranate provides good amount of protein. The
average protein content of fruit is 1.67%. Aspartic acid, Arginine and glutamine are
the predominant amino acid in pomegranate followed by glutamic acid and histi-
dine. However, other amino acids such as alanine, tryptophan, methionine, valine,
phenylalanine, isoleucine, lysine are also present. In addition, leucine and glycine
are present in trace elements (Kushwaha et al., 2013) (Table 2).
• Lipid
Pomegranate are low in fats (0.72 g/100 g of fruit weight). Both unsaturated
(0.14 g) as well as saturated fats (0.19 g) are present in the fruit with saturated
triglycerides being the predominant. The concentration of ω-3 (linoleic acid) and
oleic acid in pomegranate arils is 0.050 g/100 g and 0.048 g/100 g, respectively
(Fadavi et al., 2006).
• Vitamins and minerals
Pomegranate is an excellent source of fat soluble vitamin phylloquinone (vitamin
K), anti-coagulating and anti-haemorrhagic vitamin and provide tocopherol (vitamin
E), anti-sterility vitamin, too. B complex vitamins such as pantothenic acid (vitamin
B5) and folic acid (vitamin B9) are also present in pomegranate in good quantities.
In addition, thiamine, riboﬂavin and niacin are present in traces. Unlike most of the
fruits, pomegranate is a relatively poor source of ascorbic acid (Kushwaha et al.,
2013).
The rich mineral proﬁle of pomegranate consists of magnesium, potassium, phos-
phorus, calcium and sodium. Potassium is the major element of the fruit whereas
iron, zinc, manganese and selenium are also present. Selenium is an important trace

• Phytochemicals
Over 100 phytochemicals have been identiﬁed in pomegranate fruit. Phenolic
compounds of pomegranate include simple molecules (phenolic acids, phenyl-
propanoids, tannins) or highly polymerized compounds (lignans, melanin, tannins).
Pomegranate is considered a tannin-rich fruit and constitute hydrolyzable tannins
such as punicalin, Peduneulagin and pumicalagin (Elfalleh et al., 2011).
Among all phenols, ﬂavonoids, a 15-Carbon low molecular compound, is present
in abundance by phenolic acid which is characterized by pressure of aromatic ring
attached to one or more hydrogenated substitute. Flavonoids of pomegranate are
luteolin and quercetin (Newman et al., 2007).
Anthocyanins, the largest ﬂavonoid sub group, is responsible for colour of peel as
well as skin. The major anthocyanins of Pomegranate are cyanidins (cyanidin-3-0-
glucoside, cyaniding-3, 5-di-O glucoside, delphinidin (delphinidin-3-O-glucoside,
delphinidin-3, 5,-di-O glucoside) and pelargonidin (pelargonidin-3-O-glucoside).
Phenolic acids present in pomegranate are categorized as hydroxy benzoic acid
(ellagic, gallic acid) and Hydroxy Cinnamic acid (caffeic, chlorogenic acid and
P-coumaric acid) (Sharma & Maity, 2010).

5
Health Beneﬁts
The bioactivities of compounds (including tannins, ﬂavonoids, alkaloids, organic
acids, triterpenes and steroids, etc. are responsible for pharmaceutical, pharma-
cological and medicinal beneﬁts of pomegranate (Viuda-Martos et al., 2010a)
(Fig. 5).
• Anti-diabetic effect
World Health Organisation has listed diabetes as third most pervasive disease
after CVD and oncological disorders. Dietary interventions can play a major role
in curbing rising levels of diabetes in population. Consuming pomegranate juice
lowers blood glucose levels by blocking the activity of α-glucosidase (Goldenberg
& Punthakee, 2013).
Pomegranate compounds with potential hypoglycaemic effect are oleanolic,
ursolic and gallic acids. They limit the spread of diabetes by binding peroxisome
proliferator-activated receptor γ (as ligands of this gene increase insulin sensitivity)
and stabilizes nitric oxide production rates. Abnormal production of nitric oxide
in body is linked with diabetes (Khalil, 2004). Animal model tests have showed
that ethanolic extracts of pomegranate have potent hypoglycaemic activity in a dose
dependent manner (Nogueira & Pereira, 1986).
Polyphenols of pomegranate exert hypoglycaemic activity by altering the blood
glucose levels. Polyphenols inhibit the uptake of glucose by peripheral tissues via
blocking the activity of gut glucosidase which is essential in glucose metabolism
(Nogueira & Pereira, 1986).

• Improving skin health
Ageing of skin is a natural process, However certain factors accelerators the
process, e.g. prolonged and exposure (UV radiations), oxidative stress and immune
suppression. Polyphenols cause down regulation in formation of intracellular ROS
and increase in intracellular antioxidant activity. Oral administration of pomegranate
juice has whitening effect on skin due to impediment in proliferation of melanin
inside melanocytes (Chidambara Murthy et al., 2004).
• Anti-microbial
Pomegranate has proven bactericidal effects against Staphylococcus aureus,
Proteus vulgaris, E. coli, Bacillus subtilis and salmonella typhi. The antimicrobial
effect of pomegranate fruit is attributed to its phenols, anthocyanins and tannins
content. Gallic acid strong possesses strong inhibitory effect against Salmonella
enterica via interfering in transcriptional steps of DNA formation. Different anti-
microbials have different way of action. Some work by disrupting the cell membrane
which cause leakage of vital cellular machinery while others pose hindrances in
the activity of metabolic enzymes. Tannins are considered more toxic to microbes
than the other two (phenols and anthocyanins). Tannins form stable complexes with
proteins, carbohydrates, and metal ions (Fe and Cu) and these complexes manipulate
the structure of enzyme which have a negative effect enzyme activity. Moreover,
tannins make iron unavailable to bacterial cells by chelating the ions. Metal deﬁ-
ciency has deleterious effects on microbial cells. Phenols pose toxicity to microbes
either by reacting with sulphydryl group of proteins thereby limiting the availability
of substrate to microbes or by interfering in protein synthesis (Negi & Jayaprakasha,
2003).
In a study, application of pomegranate extract proved to be effective in inhibiting
the growth and activity of Staphylococcus aureus even at low levels (Gibbons, 2004).
• Anti-inﬂammatory activity
The anti-inﬂammatory activity of pomegranate is believed to be due to punicic
acid, a conjugated fatty acid. Consuming pomegranate causes inhibition of cyclooxy-
genase and lipoxygenase enzyme system by blocking the activation of neurophil,
a type of white blood cell. Neurophil cells leads to inﬂammation and damage to
tissue, joint, bones and cartilage via production of protease, cytokinis, chemokins,
prostaglandins and ROS (Lansky & Newman, 2007).
Short-term inﬂammatory action of cells protect the body against physical injuries,
poisons and wounds. However, continual inﬂammation results in disruption in normal
physiology of body which in turn leads to asthma and arthritis. Low molecular
weight compounds such as eicosanoids and enzymatic degradation of tissue along
with several other factors cause inﬂammation. Cyclooxygenase, an enzyme which
regulates haemostatics and facilitates arachidonic acid cycle, has been linked to
anti-inﬂammatory action of pomegranate (Fan & Watanabe, 2003).

• Gastroprotective activity
Halobacterium pyrolii is the main cause of gastric ulceration. Gastric ulceration
refers to the development of sole on the lining of stomach. The gastroprotective effect
of pomegranate is due to the presence of its rich phytonutrients (saponins, tannins and
ﬂavonoids). Tannins are high molecular weight phenolic compounds which have the
abilitytoformcomplexeswithproteinspresentatthedamagedsiteofepithelialtissue.
The tannin-protein complex adheres to the stomach lining and acts as a protective
layer on the damaged tissue thus, facilitating the healing process through preventing
excessive bleeding. Moreover, pomegranate constituents (phenolics) directly prevent
the gastric ulceration via altering the surface hydrophobicity of bacterial cell thereby,
preventing the adhesion of these microbial cells to gastric lining (De Jesus et al.,
2012).
Application of methanolic extract of pomegranate in a dose dependent to media
containing H. pyroli has shown remarkable inhibition in growth and number of pyrolii
cells (Repetto & Llesuy, 2002) (Table 3).
• Antioxidant
Repressive oxidative activity of pomegranate is contributed by phenolic
compounds such as punicalagin, anthocyanins (cyanidin-3-glucosides, delphinidin)
with punicalagin being the major antioxidant mostly concentrated in peels. These
compounds undergo redox reactions as phenolic group can readily donate hydroxyl
group (OH) in presence of reducing agents. In broader sense, phenolics manifest
antioxidative action by scavenging free radicals or chelating metal ions (Negi et al.,
2003).
Presence of reductones determine the reducing property of a compound. Reduc-
tones cause elimination of free radicals by liberation of hydrogen atom. They also
interfere in peroxide formation by reacting with the precursor of peroxide ions and
blocks the lipid peroxidation reaction.

• Osteoarthritis and Chondroprotective activity
Osteoarthritis, degenerative arthritis, is a medical condition that results from
damage to joints and the underlying bones. It generally prevails in elderly and
the main etiology behind osteoarthritis are previous injury, inheritance factors or
abnormal joint or bone development. Chondroprotection refers to a set of action
which helps to retard, inhibit or repair degenerative joint injuries. Increased cytokine
productionandreducedgrowthfactorscontroldestructionandrepairofosteoarthritis.
Intake of pomegranate reduces the damage to cartilage in people suffering from
arthritis. The fruit has the capability to reduce the inﬂammation and exert chon-
droprotective activity by inhibits the enzymes that destroy the cartilage. Animal
model studies have shown that application of pomegranate extract reduced the onset
and incidence of collagen-induced arthritis. Consuming pomegranate juice has been
found to be effective in improving histopathological damages and exerting chondro
protective effects (Rasheed et al., 2010) by repairing chondrocyte damage, causing
no cell inﬂammation or proliferation in synovial ﬂuid and limiting the damage caused
to proteoglycan contents of cartilage tissue (Table 4).
• Protection against hypertension
Pomegranate juice exert inhibitory effect on serum angiotensin converting enzyme
(ACE) activity as well as cytochrome pysoenzymes activity that facilitate “ACE”
inhibition. ACE activity causes elevated blood pressure levels and accelerates the
arte of atherosclerosis. Hence, intake of pomegranate juice reduces hypertension.
The hypotensive action of pomegranate is attributed to its antioxidant proﬁle such
as tannins. Tannins are natural polyphenols, which are able to precipitate water-
soluble alkaloids and exhibit anti hypertensive action by inhibiting the angiotensin
converting enzyme (ACE).
• Protection against cardiovascular diseases
Elevated LDL and reduced HDL are the major etiology behind CVD occurence.
Besides contributing to artheroseclorosis, LDL undergoes oxidation and causes
deposition of fats on the artery walls. Oxidized LDL is more readily used by
macrophages, white blood cells that remove cellular debris. Pomegranate maintains
cardiovascular health by manipulating with metabolism of macrophage cholesterol
via interfering with the uptake of oxidized LDL by macrophages and blocking the
cholesterol biosynthesis. Both the steps eventually lead to decrease in macrophage
